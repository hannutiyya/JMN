Editorial leadership

Approval chains, standards, legal review, investigations, corrections and transparency.

1.1  Setting up the newsroom structure1.2  Workflow and approval chains1.3  Standards, corrections and transparency1.4  Legal review and media law1.5  Investigations1.6  Elections1.7  Breaking news policy1.8  The newsroom at night and at weekends1.9  Measuring editorial quality

This chapter is for the people who answer for what the outlet publishes: the editor-in-chief, managing editors, desk chiefs, the standards editor and legal advisers. It explains the controls Sham MediaOS gives them and how to set them up so that the newsroom can move fast without losing control.

1.1Setting up the newsroom structure

Before the first story, decide how the newsroom is organised. Sham MediaOS models it in five layers, and every permission can be limited to one of them.

Layer Example Where it is set
Organisation Horn Daily Media PLC Administration → Settings
Bureau Addis Ababa, Nairobi, Mogadishu Administration → Settings
Desk Politics, Business, Sport, Video Site structure and Administration
Edition (language) English, Amharic, Afaan Oromoo Editions
Section Politics, Opinion, Health Site structure

Keep it simple at the start

Start with one bureau and the desks you really have. You can add bureaus, desks and editions later without moving stories. Over-detailed structures make permissions hard to understand.

Who should have which role

Give each person the narrowest role that lets them do their job, limited to the desk or edition they work on. Typical assignments:

Person Role Scope
Editor-in-chief Editor-in-Chief Organisation
Managing editor Managing Editor Organisation
Desk chief, Politics Desk Editor Desk: Politics
Amharic edition editor Senior Editor Language: Amharic
Reporter Reporter Desk
Translator into Afaan Oromoo Translator Language: Afaan Oromoo
Standards editor Standards Editor Organisation
Lawyer Legal Counsel Organisation
Freelance correspondent for three months Correspondent Desk, with an expiry date

The complete list of roles and their permissions is in the Administrator Manual, chapter "Roles and permissions reference".

1.2Workflow and approval chains

Every story moves through these states:

Draft

Being written. Only the author and editors of the desk can see it.

Submitted

Waiting for an editor. The author can no longer change it.

In edit

An editor is working on it. Queries go back to the author.

Awaiting approval

An approval chain applies; named approvers must sign off.

Scheduled

Approved and set to publish at a time.

Published

Live. Updates and corrections are recorded.

An approval chain is a rule: stories of this kind need approval from these roles, in this order, before they can be published. Use chains for the stories where a mistake costs most.

Kind of story Suggested chain
Investigations Desk editor → Legal counsel → Editor-in-chief
Election results stories Elections & Data Editor → Managing editor
Sponsored content Sales or advertising manager → Standards editor
Stories naming a minor or a victim of sexual violence Desk chief → Standards editor
Breaking alerts to all editions Second editor confirmation

To create an approval chain

  1. Open Administration → Approval chains and select New chain.
  2. Name it and choose when it applies: story type, desk, section, tags, edition, or a flag the author sets (for example Legal sensitive).
  3. Add the approval steps in order and choose the role for each. Set whether the author may approve their own story (normally no).
  4. Save. From now on, matching stories cannot be published until each step is approved, and every approval is recorded in the audit log.

Chains and breaking news

A chain also applies in a hurry. For stories that must go out in minutes, make sure enough people hold each approving role and are reachable, or use a separate chain for breaking news with fewer steps.

1.3Standards, corrections and transparency

Trust is built in public. Sham MediaOS gives each outlet the pages and records that show readers how it works.

  • Editorial standards, Corrections policy, Ethics and About are fixed pages per edition, edited under Pages.
  • Corrections: every correction published on a story is listed on the public corrections page with the date and the note. Corrections cannot be removed silently; removing one is itself recorded.
  • Complaints: readers' complaints arrive with a reference and the deadline set by your policy. Track them to closure and publish an annual summary if your policy requires it.
  • Transparency: ownership, funding, staff and the AI policy.
  • Journalism Trust Initiative: Administration → Compliance → JTI holds the outlet's self-assessment against the JTI standard (ISO-style CEN Workshop Agreement 17493), ready for certification.

A corrections workflow that works

  1. Anyone in the newsroom who learns of a possible error tells the standards editor through a note on the story.
  2. The standards editor decides within the day and asks the desk editor to publish the correction.
  3. The desk editor uses Add correction, not a silent edit.
  4. If the error came from a reader's complaint, the complaint is linked and closed with the outcome.

1.4Legal review and media law

Legal counsel see stories in their queue with the legal notes kept apart from the text, so that privileged advice is not published by mistake. Use Administration → Compliance → Media law to record requests from authorities, court orders, right-of-reply requests and their deadlines.

Legal holds

When litigation is expected, place a legal hold (Enterprise) on the stories, media and records concerned. Held material cannot be deleted, even by administrators, until the hold is released, and every action on it is recorded.

1.5Investigations

An investigation is a closed workspace. Only named members can see it; even administrators without membership cannot read its content, and the material is never sent to outside AI services.

To open an investigation

  1. Open Investigations and select New investigation. Give it a code name that reveals nothing.
  2. Add members one by one. Keep the list short.
  3. Upload documents; record their source and how they were obtained in the source notes, visible only to members.
  4. Build the timeline and link entities (people, companies, places).
  5. When publishing, write the stories in Story Studio, linked to the investigation, and send them through the investigation approval chain.

Source protection

Never put a confidential source's name in a story draft, a chat or an e-mail. Use the source notes inside the investigation, which are encrypted and restricted to members.

1.6Elections

Election coverage combines editorial and data work. The election editor sets up the election before polling day; results staff enter results as they are verified; the website, widgets, feeds and broadcast graphics update from the same data.

To prepare an election

  1. Open Elections and select New election. Give the name, date and editions.
  2. Add the contests (for example president, parliament by constituency), the candidates and the parties with their colours.
  3. Give the results entry permission to the staff who will enter results, and decide who verifies.
  4. Test with practice results; delete them before polling day.
  5. On the day, results entered by one person are published only after a second person verifies them.

1.7Breaking news policy

Decide in advance who may send breaking alerts, to which editions and channels, and whether a second editor must confirm. Configure it once in Administration and write it down for the newsroom.

Question Typical answer
Who may send to one edition? Edition editors and duty editors
Who may send to all editions? Managing editor or duty editor, with a second confirmation
Which channels? Website banner and app push always; SMS only for the most important events
When does an alert end? When the story is no longer developing, or after a fixed time

1.8The newsroom at night and at weekends

Set a duty editor rota in the calendar. Give duty editors the publish and breaking permissions for all editions, limited in time with expiry dates if the rota rotates. The command centre shows the duty editor what is waiting.

1.9Measuring editorial quality

  • Story analytics shows readers, reading time and sources for each story. Reading time is a better sign of quality than page views.
  • Corrections per month and complaints per month, by desk, show where checking needs attention.
  • Time to publish from submission to publication shows bottlenecks in editing and approval.
  • Edition parity (how many stories each edition publishes) shows whether languages are served equally.

This chapter is for executive producers, producers, presenters, studio operators, video editors and election producers. Broadcast features need the Broadcast edition or higher; podcasts and elections need Professional or higher.

2.1How broadcast fits together

Channels and schedule

Each radio or television channel has a programme schedule that is shown on the website's Schedule and Watch pages.

Rundowns

Each programme on a date has a rundown: the running order of items with timings, scripts and media.

Studios

A studio collects sources: cameras, microphones, remote guests and playout. Broadcast includes 3 studios, Enterprise 20.

Destinations

Where the programme goes: the website and apps, and outside platforms, at the same time.

Graphics

Lower thirds, tickers and full-screen graphics sent to an overlay page used by the vision mixer or streaming software.

Interaction

Audience questions, votes and messages, moderated and shown to the presenter.

2.2Setting up a channel and its studios

Who does this

A broadcast engineer or the executive producer, once, with an administrator.

Steps

  1. Open Broadcast → Setup and create the channel: name, type (radio or television), editions and logo.
  2. Open Broadcast → Studios and create each studio. Name the sources the studio uses.
  3. Open Broadcast → Destinations and add where the stream goes. Each outside platform needs a stream key from that platform; it is stored encrypted.
  4. If you use the studio desktop app on Windows, install it on the studio computer and pair it with the code shown in Setup.
  5. Open Broadcast → Schedule and enter the programmes with their days and times.

2.3Building a rundown

Steps

  1. Open Broadcast → Rundowns and select the programme and date. A new rundown can start from a template of the programme.
  2. Add items in running order: intro, headlines, packages, live interviews, weather, breaks, closing.
  3. For each item set the planned duration, the presenter, the camera or source, and the script. Link a story to reuse its text and media.
  4. Watch the total at the top: over-time items are shown in red against the programme's slot.
  5. Lock the rundown before air if the executive producer wants changes to be approved.

Timing

Plan 10 to 15 seconds of buffer for each live item. The rundown shows back time (when each item must start to end on time), so the director can drop or shorten an item while on air.

2.4On air: prompter, presenter view and graphics

  • Prompter: opens the scripts of the rundown on a tablet or a screen in front of the camera. The operator controls the speed; the presenter can read it from any device with a browser.
  • Presenter view: the approved audience questions and messages, large and clear.
  • Graphics: operators fire lower thirds (name and role of a speaker), tickers and full-screen graphics. The overlay page shows them over the programme in the vision mixer or the streaming software (for example OBS or vMix as a browser source).

During the programme

  1. The director opens the rundown in On air mode.
  2. As each item ends, the director marks it done; the next item and its back time are highlighted.
  3. The graphics operator fires the lower third for each speaker from the item.
  4. The producer moderates audience messages in the interaction session and approves the ones for the presenter.

2.5Remote guests

Remote guests join from a browser link, without installing anything. Sham MediaOS uses a self-hosted LiveKit gateway by default (Jitsi is the second option), so the call stays on the outlet's own server.

To bring in a remote guest

  1. Open Studio → Remote guests and select Invite guest.
  2. Enter the guest's name and the programme. Send the link by e-mail or a messaging app.
  3. The guest opens the link, allows the camera and microphone and waits in the green room.
  4. The producer checks sound and picture and brings the guest into the studio when the item starts.

Before a live interview

Test the guest's connection at least 15 minutes before air. Ask the guest to use a cable or a strong Wi-Fi signal, close other apps and use headphones to prevent echo.

2.6Live video events

A live event streams to the website, apps and outside platforms. Create it under Live, link it to a studio or a remote source, choose the destinations, and go live. The website shows the player on the live page and on the Watch page. When the event ends, the recording can be published as a video.

2.7Podcasts

To publish a podcast episode

  1. Open Podcasts and select the show (or create one with artwork of at least 1400 × 1400 pixels, a description, categories and language).
  2. Select New episode and upload the audio.
  3. Write the title, description and show notes; add chapters and a transcript.
  4. Choose Publish now or a time. The episode appears on the website and in the show's feed, which podcast platforms read automatically.

Transcripts

Transcripts make episodes searchable and accessible. Where the AI desk is switched on, a transcript can be drafted automatically; a person should check names and numbers before publishing.

2.8Video clips and vertical video

The Video workspace turns long video into clips for the website and social platforms: choose in and out points, the format (horizontal, square or vertical), captions and branding, then publish to the website and upload to the connected platforms. Each upload is recorded with its result.

2.9Election night

Election nights combine the newsroom, the website and the studio.

Election night checklist

  1. Before polls close: results staff are signed in with two-step verification; verifiers are assigned; the results pages and widgets are on the front page; the election graphics are loaded in the studio.
  2. As results come in: one person enters, a second verifies; only verified results are published.
  3. The website, embedded widgets, feeds and the Election graphics in the studio update from the same verified data.
  4. Presenters read from the presenter view and the rundown.
  5. After the night: check the final figures against the official declaration and mark the contests final.

2.10Audience interaction in programmes

Create an interaction session for a programme to collect questions, votes and messages from the website, apps and SMS. Producers approve messages; approved items appear in the presenter view and can be shown on screen as a graphic. Votes show live results.

2.11Troubleshooting on air

Symptom Likely cause What to do
Guest has no sound Browser blocked the microphone, or wrong device Ask the guest to allow the microphone in the browser bar and choose the right device
Stream not visible on the website Destination off, or encoder not sending Check Destinations and the studio status; restart the encoder
Graphics do not appear in the mixer Overlay page not loaded as a browser source Reload the browser source; check the overlay address for the channel

3.1An audience strategy in Sham MediaOS

Readers come back when they get something useful, in their language, on the channel they already use. Sham MediaOS gives each edition its own channels:

Channel Best for Where
Website and light edition Everyone, including slow connections Website
Apps Loyal readers, notifications, offline reading App Studio
Newsletters Habit and loyalty; the best path to membership Newsletters
Push notifications Breaking news and followed topics Notifications
SMS and USSD Basic phones and areas with poor data SMS
Messaging channels Readers who live in Telegram or WhatsApp Messaging
Social platforms Discovery by new readers Social, Video
Podcasts Listening on the move Podcasts

3.2Newsletters

Designing a newsletter programme

Start with one daily briefing per edition and one or two topic newsletters with a clear promise (for example Business in the Horn, every Monday). Add more only when you can keep them going.

To create a newsletter list

  1. Open Newsletters and select New list.
  2. Give the name and description in each edition's language, the sending day and time, and the sender name.
  3. Choose automatic (issues assembled from the latest stories in chosen sections) or hand-made.
  4. Place the sign-up form on the front page and at the end of stories.
  5. Readers confirm their subscription by e-mail (double opt-in); only confirmed addresses receive issues.

Deliverability

Watch Newsletters → Deliverability every week. A rising bounce or complaint rate hurts all your e-mail.

  • Hard bounces (address does not exist) are suppressed automatically.
  • Complaints (a reader marked the message as spam) unsubscribe that reader automatically.
  • Keep complaints below 0.1 % and bounces below 2 % of messages sent.
  • Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC for your sending domain; the administrator's guide explains how.

3.3Push notifications

Send push only for news readers would want to be interrupted for. Use topics so readers choose what they receive, and look at the opening rate per notification to learn what works. Too many notifications make readers switch them off for good.

3.4Social publishing and video

Schedule posts per edition and platform from Social, and publish clips from Video. Link back to the full story on your own website, where readers can sign up for newsletters and support you.

3.5Callouts, tips and contributors

  • Callouts bring readers' experiences and documents into reporting. Write a clear promise of how answers will be used and keep it.
  • Tips come to the newsroom through a secure form; treat every tip as possibly sensitive.
  • Contributors have their own identity type, separate from staff and readers. Verify contributors before publishing their work, and keep payment records in their profile.

3.6Community and moderation

Write community rules and publish them as a fixed page. Choose pre-moderation (comments appear after approval) for sensitive sections and post-moderation for the rest. Moderators work from Comments, handling reported comments first. Blocked readers and the reasons are recorded.

Moderation load

One moderator can handle a few hundred comments per shift. Close comments on stories likely to attract abuse (for example about victims of crime) rather than leaving them unmoderated.

3.7Experiments

Test two or more headlines or main images for a story. Sham MediaOS shows each version to a share of readers and reports which one gets more readers to open and finish the story. Use the winner, and learn what your readers respond to over time.

3.8Reader revenue

Plans and prices

To set up memberships

  1. With the administrator, connect at least one payment method under Administration → Integrations (Stripe for cards, PayPal, Chapa, telebirr, ArifPay or bank transfer). Each uses the outlet's own merchant account.
  2. Open Revenue and create plans: name, benefits, prices per currency, monthly and yearly.
  3. Decide the paywall: which sections are for members, how many free stories per month, and which stories are always free (breaking news, public interest).
  4. Place the Support button and the membership offer on the website.
  5. Check Revenue reports weekly: new members, cancellations, revenue per plan and per channel.

Payments are confirmed by the provider

A membership becomes active only when the payment provider confirms the payment to the server. A refunded payment ends the benefit it paid for. Bank transfers are activated when a staff member records the transfer.

Keeping members

  • Send a welcome newsletter to new members with what they now get.
  • Show members what their support paid for: a yearly letter from the editor works well.
  • Watch failed renewals; readers whose card expired are often happy to update it when asked.

3.9Advertising

The advertising module manages direct sales: advertisers, campaigns, creatives and placements.

To run a campaign

  1. Open Advertising and add the advertiser.
  2. Create the campaign with its dates, editions, placements, impression goal and frequency limit.
  3. Upload the creatives in the sizes of each placement; add the click address.
  4. Activate. The campaign ends by itself at its end date or impression goal.
  5. Share the campaign report with the advertiser.

Mark sponsored content clearly. Sponsored newsletters and sponsored stories carry a visible label, and an approval chain can require the standards editor to approve them.

3.10Analytics

Sham MediaOS measures audiences without third-party trackers. The main measures:

Measure What it tells you
Readers People who opened at least one page, counted without following them across other sites
Page views Pages opened
Reading time How long readers stay with a story; the best sign of value
Completion Share of readers who reached the end of a story
Sources Where readers came from: search, social, newsletters, apps, direct
Returning readers Readers who came back within the period
Conversions Newsletter sign-ups and memberships started from a story

Look at story analytics the day after publication, and at edition analytics every week. Compare editions to see whether each language gets the coverage its readers need.

4.1Breaking News Control Center

/newsroom/breaking (API GET|POST /newsroom/breaking, POST /newsroom/breaking/{id}/extend|end). Sending a breaking alert is one deliberate action for one published story in one edition. Nothing is ever published from here.

Channel What happens Gate
Breaking bar (always) The story leads the edition's ticker with the alert headline (editable, max 160 characters) article.publish_breaking for the edition
Homepage lead The story is pinned as the hero lead while the alert is active same
Social accounts A BREAKING: … post is queued on each active account of that edition social.manage for the edition, otherwise skipped with the reason
Breaking newsletter A draft on the edition's breaking list, for review (never auto-sent from here; idempotent per story) newsletter.manage for the edition
Live coverage Links the alert to a live video of the same edition —
Web push / WhatsApp Recorded as skipped: not configured until a provider is connected (P2). Never shown as sent. —
  • Strict permissions: article.publish_breaking (sensitive) for the edition, plus an MFA-verified session (a token without the mfa ability gets 403, even for the Editor-in-Chief). Each send is audited (breaking.alert_sent) and recorded as a security event (breaking_alert). Language-scoped editors see and alert only their editions.
  • Time-limited: default 6 h, maximum 48 h from the start. Alerts can be extended or ended (optimistic lock). jmn:breaking:expire runs every minute, and the desk also expires due alerts when it loads. Ended alerts stay on record with the reason (expired or editor). articles.is_breaking is set while some edition has an active alert and cleared afterwards.
  • One active alert per story per edition (a partial unique index, plus 409 ALREADY_BREAKING).
  • Every channel writes a delivery row (breaking_alert_deliveries: done / queued / skipped / failed, with target and reason), so the desk sees exactly where an alert went and where it did not.
  • Behaviour change: the public breaking bar used to list every story ever flagged is_breaking, so it never emptied. It now shows only active alerts. Send and end both trigger revalidation of breaking, home:{lang}, layout:{lang} and article:{id}. When breaking is among the tags, the web expires the pages immediately ({ expire: 0 }, not stale-while-revalidate), so the very next reader already sees the alert, and sees it gone once it ends (verified: first request after send and after end).
  • Tests: BreakingNewsTest (4 API) and e2e/breaking.spec.ts (send → reader bar shows it → end; a reporter has no desk).

4.2Desk collaboration and the readiness gate

Full design and audit: doc 22.

  • Notes and queries: workflow_comments are per story, optionally per edition, and immutable. A query is a must-fix item.
  • The engine refuses published, scheduled and updated while any query is open (409 READINESS_BLOCKED).
  • The story's authors, the asker or a reviewer may resolve it. The asker is told.
  • Mentions: @handle (the e-mail local part) only reaches colleagues who can already see the story. Anyone else is ignored silently.
  • Tasks: workflow_tasks move open → in_progress → done | cancelled, with lock_version.
  • The assignee must hold the task's permission in scope (table in doc 22 §4).
  • Unassigned tasks are claimable by anyone who could do them.
  • There is one open task per type and edition (409 TASK_EXISTS).
  • Readiness (GET articles/{id}/readiness?language=):
  • Blockers: headline, body, open queries, lead-image alt, expired image licence.
  • Warnings: summary, SEO title length, image size and credit, section, byline, topics, open tasks, body-image alt, insecure links, possible duplicate, short body.

Rules (enforced in the API)

  • Figures are four-eyes. Entered or imported figures are pending and invisible to readers until someone else with election.verify verifies them (the database also refuses self-verification).   • Calls are always four-eyes. election.enter proposes (with its basis), a different election.verify holder approves. A MediaOS projection needs verified figures; an official declaration does not. Retracting a call is an uncall that goes through the same approval.
  • Neutral presentation. Candidates are shown in ballot order until votes are counted, then by votes. Candidates without a party share one neutral grey. A race without an approved call shows “Not called” with “Figures can change as more votes are counted”, whoever leads. Every figure shows its source, its as-of time and the reporting percentage; the page says figures were checked by a second journalist and are provisional until certified.

Signing published images

  • After ProcessImage makes the renditions, SignContentCredentials (media queue) signs every public rendition (JPEG, WebP, AVIF) in place: c2patool --settings <thumbnails off> <in> -m manifest.json -o <out>. The manifest: c2pa.actions (opened the processed rendition, resized, transcoded, published, software agent “Sham MediaOS Newsroom”), stds.schema-org.CreativeWork (photographer, copyright, credit, capture date, publisher “Sham MediaOS”, edition), and com.jmnglobal.publication (publisher, site, edition, asset id, credit).
  • Originals are never signed and never ingredients — the uploaded file’s camera metadata/GPS cannot leak into a public manifest. Thumbnails are disabled; a signed rendition grows by the manifest only (≈10–15 KB with a two-certificate chain).
  • State per asset in media_content_credentials (pending | signed | partial | failed | not_configured | skipped, signer, actions, error). Re-processing an image clears the state and re-signs. Public image descriptors carry credentials only when all renditions are signed; the web then shows the Content Credentials mark (a no-script disclosure with publisher, edition, what MediaOS did, signing date and a link to contentcredentials.org/verify) under lead and body images.
  • Automatic after processing when configured (C2PA_AUTO_SIGN, default true); manual from the media library (provenance.manage) or php artisan jmn:c2pa:sign --asset=… | --unsigned [--queue].

Validating incoming files (fact-check verification toolkit)

The API host now reads C2PA itself (c2patool <file> [trust --trust_anchors …]) when an evidence file is uploaded and on demand (“Check credentials again”, POST /newsroom/fact-checks/{id}/files/{file}/c2pa), so provenance is checked even without the AI worker. The result separates integrity (valid | invalid | unverified | absent) from trust (trusted | untrusted | unknown against C2PA_TRUST_ANCHORS) and flags files signed with MediaOS’s own certificate. The worker’s result is kept unless it could not validate.

7.4YouTube (YouTube Data API v3)

  • OAuth: authorization-code flow at accounts.google.com/o/oauth2/v2/auth with access_type=offline, prompt=consent; scopes youtube.upload (videos.insert) and youtube.force-ssl (captions.insert). Token exchange and refresh at oauth2.googleapis.com/token; Google keeps the refresh token across refreshes. The channel id comes from channels.list?mine=true. Disconnect revokes at oauth2.googleapis.com/revoke.
  • Upload: resumable protocol — POST upload/youtube/v3/videos?uploadType=resumable&part=snippet,status with X-Upload-Content-Length/-Type, then PUT chunks (multiple of 256 KiB, JMN_YOUTUBE_CHUNK_BYTES, default 8 MiB) with Content-Range. A 308 carries the bytes received; after a network error or 5xx the next attempt asks with an empty PUT (Content-Range: bytes */size) and resumes from there; a 404 means the session expired, so it starts again.
  • Per edition: title (≤ 100 characters, no </>), description (≤ 5,000 bytes), tags (≤ 500 characters), privacy (private / unlisted / public, chosen by the editor), made for children (required answer), notify subscribers, category (default 25 News & Politics), default language = the edition.
  • Captions: after the video is accepted, the clip's published track in the channel's edition is sent with captions.insert (multipart). A caption failure does not fail the upload; it is shown on the upload.
  • Quota: videos.insert has its own bucket (the YouTube docs list "100 calls per day"); captions.insert costs 400 units of the normal daily quota. Ask Google for more quota before heavy use. The current videos.insert reference states that videos from unverified API projects are not restricted to private viewing; the OAuth consent screen still has to be verified by Google for the sensitive scopes before people outside your test-user list can connect.
  • Vertical clips get a youtube.com/shorts/{id} link, others watch?v={id}.

7.5TikTok (Content Posting API)

  • OAuth (Login Kit v2): www.tiktok.com/v2/auth/authorize/ with scopes user.info.basic,video.publish,video.upload; token and refresh at open.tiktokapis.com/v2/oauth/token/ (access token 24 h, refresh token 365 days and may rotate — the new one is stored). Identity from /v2/user/info/. Disconnect calls /v2/oauth/revoke/.
  • Mandatory UX (TikTok content sharing guidelines), enforced in the form and the API: creator info (/v2/post/publish/creator_info/query/) is re-read every time the form opens and shows the creator's nickname; privacy is chosen from that account's privacy_level_options with no default; comment/duet/stitch are off unless switched on, and greyed out where the creator disabled them; commercial-content disclosure with Your brand / Branded content (one required when the toggle is on; branded content can't be Only me; the "Promotional content" / "Paid partnership" labels are shown); the Music Usage Confirmation (and Branded Content Policy) declaration is shown and required; the clip length is checked against max_video_post_duration_sec; a preview is shown; nothing is sent before consent and approval; readers are told processing takes a few minutes.
  • Modes: Direct Post (/v2/post/publish/video/init/, video.publish) or upload to the creator's inbox (/v2/post/publish/inbox/video/init/, video.upload; the creator finishes in the TikTok app).
  • Transfer: FILE_UPLOAD, chunks of 5–64 MB in order (JMN_TIKTOK_CHUNK_BYTES, default 10 MB; files under 5 MB in one piece; the last chunk takes the remainder), PUT to the returned upload URL with Content-Range. An expired/out-of-sync URL (403/404/416) starts a new post on the next attempt.
  • Status: PollVideoUpload calls /v2/post/publish/status/fetch/ with back-off until PUBLISH_COMPLETE (link built from publicaly_available_post_id once public), SEND_TO_USER_INBOX, or FAILED (reason shown); gives up after 6 hours.
  • Audit: until TikTok audits the app, Direct Post works only with SELF_ONLY on private accounts and for at most 5 posting users in 24 hours; the form then offers only "Only me" and says why (JMN_TIKTOK_AUDITED=false). 8.1Reader callouts

What it does. An editor writes a question ("Tell us"), up to 15 structured questions (long or short answer, one or several choices, yes/no, place, date, number), the consent text readers agree to, a closing date and a retention period. The editor opens the callout. It then has its own page, and the editor can embed it in any story of the same edition with a Reader callout block in Story Studio (paste the callout id from the desk).

Intake (POST /api/v1/engagement/{code}/callouts/{slug}/answers, no account needed): - Throttled at 20/min on the route. CalloutService also allows 8 per hour per network address and 4 per day per email. The address is stored only as an HMAC (keyed with APP_KEY). - A filled honeypot field (website) gets the same 201 response as a real answer, and nothing is stored. - Likely spam is flagged, not rejected. A submission is flagged when it has more than 3 links (many_links) or repeats the same text within 24 h (duplicate). Since 4 Nov 2026 (T16) it is also flagged (blocked_term) when it contains one of the edition's blocked terms (Comment moderation → Blocked terms); answers are never refused for a term. With Cloudflare Turnstile on (doc 28 §3.7), the form also needs a valid bot-check token. Flagged answers go to the desk's Possible spam tab and send no inbox alert. - Under-16 rule (PDPP 1321/2024 Art. 11, the same rule as reader accounts): the form asks "How old are you?". If the reader answers under 16, the form tells them a parent or guardian must answer for them, and nothing is sent. The API also refuses age=minor with 422 GUARDIAN_REQUIRED and keeps nothing. - Consent is recorded with each answer: consented_at, the SHA-256 of the exact consent text shown, age_confirmed, and the reader's choices may contact and may publish my name. A check constraint makes every answer that has not been purged carry this consent record.

Reader files (added 4 Nov 2026, T15). A question of type File (photo, PDF, audio, video) lets readers attach up to one file per question (at most 3 file questions per callout). - Offered only while the malware scanner is configured (JMN_CLAMAV_HOST or JMN_CLAMAV_SOCKET, doc 28 §3.6). Without it the desk refuses new file questions (scanner_required), and existing ones are hidden from readers and no longer required. - The browser sends the file first, through the same-origin route /api/callout-file/{lang}/{slug} to POST /api/v1/engagement/{code}/callouts/{slug}/files (throttle:10,1, plus JMN_CALLOUT_FILES_PER_IP_HOUR, 10, per hashed address). - The type is sniffed from the bytes: JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, PDF, MP3, M4A, Ogg, WAV, MP4, MOV, WebM. Size ≤ JMN_CALLOUT_FILE_MAX_BYTES (20 MB, at most 50 MB). Office documents and archives stay with GlobaLeaks. - The scan fails closed. An infected file is quarantined and refused (MALWARE_FOUND). If the scanner cannot answer, the upload is refused (SCAN_UNAVAILABLE). - The file is stored encrypted with APP_KEY on the private disk; its name is encrypted too. Metadata (EXIF, GPS) is kept for verification and is never published. The reader gets a one-time token; the answer carries it, and the file is attached to that answer only (a token works once, for its own callout and question). Files no answer claimed are deleted after 2 hours by jmn:callouts:sweep. - On the desk, everyone with callouts.view sees file names and sizes. Only callouts.manage can download (GET /newsroom/callout-files/{id}, audited callout.file_downloaded), always as an attachment (application/octet-stream, nosniff, sandboxed), never shown in the browser. - Files go with their answer: retention purge, erasure requests and the desk's purge delete them.

Desk (callouts.view / callouts.manage, scoped per edition): - callouts.view: reads answers, but email and phone are masked. - callouts.manage: sees contact details and can create, open, close and edit callouts. It can also triage answers: set the status (new → reviewing / contacted / used / discarded), assign the answer to someone who also runs callouts for that edition, add an encrypted desk note, mark or clear spam, and keep for a story (a reason is required). - contacted is only allowed when the reader agreed to be contacted. - Every write uses lock_version (409 EDIT_CONFLICT) and goes to the hash-chained audit log. The immutable callout_submission_events log holds metadata only (status, assignee id, note length), so purging an answer leaves no personal data behind. - Once readers have answered, a question cannot change its type or be removed. The API returns 409 FIELDS_IN_USE. Labels, help text and options can still change. - A new answer that is not flagged sends an inbox notice (TrustNotice kind callout) to people who run callouts for that edition.

PII minimisation and retention: - Answers, name, email, phone and the desk note are encrypted at rest. - retain_until is set to the closing date plus retention_days (30–730; default JMN_CALLOUT_RETENTION_DAYS, 180). If the callout has no closing date, it counts from receipt. Closing early, or changing the closing date or the retention period, recomputes it for every live answer. - php artisan jmn:callouts:sweep runs hourly. It closes callouts whose closing time has passed. It also purges answers past retain_until that nobody kept for a story: every personal column and hash is set to null and a tombstone stays. The check constraint callout_submissions_purged_empty proves a purged row is empty. - Media-law and compliance tie-in. - The record of processing has two new baseline entries: callouts (legal basis consent) and secure_tips (journalism, DPIA required). Load them with Administration → Compliance → Load baseline. - Data-subject requests now cover callout answers. The person's email hash is used to find them; export includes them; erasure purges them, including answers kept for a story, because consent was the legal basis.

9.2Architecture

                                                         └▶ adapter.startCheckout ─▶ hosted page (Chapa / Stripe) | bank details
provider ─▶ POST /revenue/webhooks/{chapa|stripe} ─▶ store raw event ─▶ verify signature ─▶ re-read from provider API

  • Credit rules:
  • Only a VerifiedCharge changes a payment's state. It comes from the provider API, or from a desk confirmation for bank transfers.
  • If the amount, currency or live/test mode differs from the ledger, the payment goes to needs_review and is never credited. The mismatch is audited.
  • Every transition locks the payment row, so a double webhook, the callback and the return page can race safely.
  • Adapters (PaymentGateway): TelebirrGateway and ArifPayGateway (4 Nov 2026), ChapaGateway, StripeGateway, PaypalGateway (4 Nov 2026), ManualGateway. For birr the support page offers Telebirr direct first, then Chapa, then ArifPay; abroad, Stripe (cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay) and PayPal. Each declares its currencies, its merchant entity and whether recurring is native.
  • Chapa: POST /transaction/initialize, GET /transaction/verify/{tx_ref}, POST /refund/{tx_ref}.
    • Webhooks: x-chapa-signature = HMAC-SHA256(secret, body). If it is present it must match.
    • Otherwise chapa-signature = HMAC-SHA256(secret, secret) must match.
    • The charge is always re-verified through the API.
    • callback_url is only a nudge to verify.
  • Stripe:
    • Checkout Sessions: payment mode for gifts, subscription mode with inline recurring prices for memberships.
    • Billing renewals arrive as invoice.paid. customer.subscription.* events keep state in sync, charge.refunded records refunds, and the customer portal handles self-service.
    • Stripe-Signature uses the v1 HMAC with a 300 s timestamp tolerance.
    • Every event is re-fetched with GET /v1/events/{id}, and only that copy is acted on.
    • The REST API is called directly (no SDK dependency).
  • PayPal (task T20, same merchant entity as Stripe; offered beside Stripe for USD/EUR/GBP):
    • Orders v2: intent=CAPTURE, no shipping, PayPal-Request-Id: checkout-{tx_ref}; the reader approves on PayPal.
    • The approved order is captured once (PayPal-Request-Id: capture-{tx_ref}), from the webhook or the return page, whichever comes first; the amount, currency and fee come from the order as the API returns it.
    • Webhooks (CHECKOUT.ORDER.APPROVED, PAYMENT.CAPTURE.*) are verified by PayPal itself (/v1/notifications/verify-webhook-signature with the transmission headers and PAYPAL_WEBHOOK_ID).
    • Refunds on the capture (/v2/payments/captures/{id}/refund, PayPal-Request-Id: refund-{id}); refunds made in PayPal's dashboard are confirmed through the order API before the ledger records them.
    • Soft recurring (reminders), like Chapa. Off until PAYPAL_CLIENT_ID, PAYPAL_CLIENT_SECRET and PAYPAL_WEBHOOK_ID are set; PAYPAL_MODE=sandbox|live.
  • Telebirr direct (task T20, Ethio Telecom H5 web checkout, C2B; ETB; the Ethiopian company):
    • A fabric token (POST /payment/v1/token, X-APP-Key + app secret) authorises every call and is cached 50 minutes.
    • preOrder (/payment/v1/merchant/preOrder) returns a prepay_id; the reader goes to Telebirr's web paygate with a signed raw request (appid, merch_code, nonce_str, prepay_id, timestamp). merch_order_id is the tx_ref without dashes and is stored as the checkout session id.
    • Every request is signed RSASSA-PSS (SHA-256, MGF1-SHA-256, 32-byte salt; Telebirr's "SHA256WithRSA") with our private key over the sorted key=value pairs of the request and its biz_content, minus sign, sign_type and the other unsigned fields (RsaPss, TelebirrGateway::signString).
    • Notifications (/api/v1/revenue/webhooks/telebirr) must carry a valid signature by Telebirr's public key and our merchant code and app id; they only trigger queryOrder, which alone sets status and amount (mismatch → needs_review). Idempotent on the transaction id and status.
    • Refunds through /payment/v1/merchant/refund (REFUNDING = pending). Soft recurring (reminders).
  • ArifPay (task T20, hosted checkout: Telebirr, CBE Birr, Awash, Amole, BOA, Kacha, HelloCash, M-Pesa, cards; ETB):
    • POST /api/checkout/session with the payer's mobile number (required by ArifPay; passed through for this checkout only, never stored), the items, the beneficiary bank account and a 2-hour expiry → paymentUrl (https only) and sessionId (stored as the checkout session id).
    • ArifPay does not sign notifications. The notify URL carries a per-payment HMAC token (RevenueLinks::notifyToken, APP_KEY); a notice without it is rejected, and one with it only triggers GET /api/checkout/session/{id}, which alone sets status and amount.
    • No refund API: the money goes back in the ArifPay portal and the desk records the refund (status manual).
    • Renewals of ArifPay memberships from the e-mail link land on the support page (the link carries no phone number), where the reader pays again with any rail.
  • Manual: shows bank details. The desk confirms the credit (MFA required).
  • Soft recurring (Chapa and bank): the hourly jmn:revenue:renewals job handles each stage:
  • It sends a reminder email N days before the period ends (per edition, default 7) with a signed one-click renewal link. The link creates a fresh checkout when clicked, so it never goes stale.
  • When the period ends, the membership becomes past_due and a final reminder is sent. Benefits continue for the grace period (default 7 days).
  • When grace ends, the membership becomes lapsed.
  • A renewal paid early or during grace continues the cycle; a renewal paid after a lapse starts a new one.
  • The in-app reminder is the "renewal due" notice and the Renew now button in /account.
  • Abandoned checkouts are closed after 24 h (bank transfers after 14 days).
  • Desk /newsroom/revenue (revenue.view / revenue.manage, scoped per edition):
  • KPIs: MRR by currency, active members, 30-day churn, one-off totals, figures by edition.
  • Lists: supporters (masked email, initials, exact-email search only), payments with provider status, plans, and per-edition settings and prompts.
  • Provider event log with replay. Replay is safe because the ledger is idempotent; rejected (unsigned) events cannot be replayed.
  • Payouts register, via the API.
  • Refunds, bank confirmations, CSV export and replays require an MFA-verified session. Export also needs revenue.manage and is audited.
  • New role Reader Revenue Manager (commercial, MFA). Executives, directors and partnership managers can view. Editorial roles cannot see the desk at all.
  • Emails: supporter.receipt and supporter.renewal_due (EmailCatalog), with built-in wording in en, am, ar, es, fr, tr and zh. Interface strings are in RevenueUiStrings for all eleven dictionaries.

  1. .env: JMN_AI_WORKER_URL=http://ai-worker:8090, JMN_AI_WORKER_TOKEN (generate-secrets.sh fills it), add ai to COMPOSE_PROFILES; optional HF_TOKEN of an account that accepted the Sunbird terms.
  2. pgvector: use pgvector/pgvector:pg17 (same data format as postgres:17) or install postgresql-17-pgvector, then as superuser CREATE EXTENSION vector; in the app DB before running the migrations (the app role is not a superuser). Without it the migration falls back to real[] (fine up to ~100k passages). To convert later: create the extension, ALTER TABLE semantic_chunks ALTER COLUMN embedding TYPE vector(1024) USING embedding::vector, then CREATE INDEX … USING hnsw (embedding vector_cosine_ops).
  3. Disk: ~10 GB in the ai-models volume (~14 GB with Omnilingual) + 4 GB tmpfs. First start downloads in the background; /status and the admin page show progress. Or bake: --build-arg JMN_AI_PRELOAD="multilingual-e5-large …".
  4. CPU (default): 4–8 vCPU, 12–16 GB RAM for the full set; STT at roughly 0.3–1× real time on 8 vCPU (Whisper turbo int8). GPU (recommended for backlogs / Omnilingual 7B): build with JMN_AI_BASE=nvidia/cuda:12.4.1-cudnn-runtime-ubuntu22.04, JMN_AI_DEVICE=cuda, add a compose override with deploy.resources.reservations.devices: [{capabilities: [gpu]}]; 24 GB VRAM (L4/A10/4090) fits the 7B Omnilingual LLM variant.

  5. php artisan migrate, then php artisan jmn:ai:index to embed the archive.

  6. Evaluate per language before enabling an edition (Amharic/Tigrinya WER ≈ 30–40 %): human caption QA and MT post-editing stay mandatory.
