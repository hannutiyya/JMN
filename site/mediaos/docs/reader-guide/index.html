Reader guide

Everything a reader can do, step by step.

This guide explains the outlet's website and apps to the people who read, listen and watch. An outlet can give it to readers as a help page, print it for a help desk, or use it to train the staff who answer readers' questions. The screenshots show the example outlet Horn Daily; your outlet's name, logo, colour and languages appear instead.

1.1Choosing your language

Every outlet that runs Sham MediaOS publishes one or more editions. An edition is the whole publication in one language: its own front page, its own sections and its own editors. The edition is the first part of every web address, for example /en for English, /am for Amharic, /om for Afaan Oromoo and /ar for Arabic.

To change the edition

  1. Open the language menu at the top of any page. It shows each language in its own script, for example አማርኛ for Amharic and العربية for Arabic.
  2. Choose the language you want. The site opens the front page of that edition.
  3. Your choice is remembered on this device. The next time you visit the outlet's address, you go straight to your edition.

Why a story may be missing in your language

Editions are edited separately, so not every story appears in every language. When you follow a link to a page that has no version in your edition, the site shows the English page if there is one, or tells you plainly that the page does not exist in this edition.

Arabic and other right-to-left editions mirror the whole page: the menu, the reading direction and the order of columns. Amharic and Tigrinya use Ethiopic type with line spacing designed for that script.

1.2The front page

The front page of each edition is arranged by its editors. From top to bottom you usually find:

  • The top bar with the language menu, search, sign-in and the button to support the outlet.
  • The main menu with the sections of the edition, such as News, Politics, Business, Sport, Opinion and Video.
  • A breaking news banner when something important is happening. Select it to read the story.
  • The lead stories chosen by the editors, followed by blocks for each section, live coverage, video, podcasts and most-read stories.
  • The footer with pages about the outlet, contacts, corrections, complaints, terms and privacy, newsletters and the apps.

1.3Reading a story

Select a headline to open the story. A story page shows the headline, a short summary, the author and the time it was published or last updated, the main picture or video, and the text.

  • Updates and corrections are shown at the top or the bottom of the story with the date. The outlet never changes a published fact quietly.
  • Photos and galleries open larger when selected. Swipe or use the arrow keys to move through a gallery.
  • Video and audio play inside the page. Captions are offered where the outlet has added them.
  • Related stories and the storyline at the end help you follow a developing subject.
  • Share sends the story by messaging apps, e-mail or social networks, or copies its link.
  • Save keeps the story in your account to read later (you need to be signed in).
  • Listen reads the story aloud where the outlet offers audio versions.

Text too small or too large?

Use your browser's zoom (Ctrl and + or − on a computer, or pinch on a phone). The pages are built to stay readable at any size and to work with screen readers.

1.4Searching and browsing

  • Search is in the top bar. Type words in the language of the edition you are in. Results show the newest matching stories first; you can narrow them by section or date.
  • Sections and topics collect stories on one subject. Topics can be followed from your account.
  • Authors have their own pages with their stories.
  • The archive lists every story by date. Pick a day on the calendar to see what was published that day.
  • The e-paper shows the edition as a printed-style page for a given date, where the outlet produces one.
  • The sitemap lists all sections and pages of the edition.

1.5Live coverage

When a big event is unfolding, the outlet opens a live page. New posts appear at the top on their own; you do not need to reload. Key events are listed at the side so that you can jump to them. Live video, when there is any, plays at the top of the page.

The Watch page lists live channels and recent videos, and the Schedule page shows the outlet's radio and television programmes by day.

1.6Listening: podcasts

The Podcasts page lists the outlet's shows. Each show has episodes with a player, a description and, often, a transcript. To listen in your favourite podcast app, copy the show's feed address from the show page, or use the buttons for the podcast platforms the outlet lists.

1.7Elections

During elections the Elections pages show contests, candidates and results as they are verified by the newsroom. Results are marked with the share of polling stations counted and the time of the last update. The outlet may also show results maps and allow partners to embed the results on their own sites.

1.8Your reader account

You can read most of the site without an account. An account lets you save stories, follow topics, manage newsletters and notifications, comment, and manage a membership.

To create an account or sign in

  1. Select Sign in in the top bar.
  2. Enter your e-mail address (or your phone number, where the outlet allows sign-in by SMS).
  3. Open the message the outlet sends you and select the link, or type the code it contains. There is no password to remember.
  4. The first time, choose what you agree to: newsletters, personalised recommendations and analytics. You can change these choices at any time.

From Account you can see your saved stories and followed topics, your newsletters, your notification settings, your membership and payments, and your privacy choices. You can also download a copy of your data or delete your account; deleting it removes your personal details and signs you out on every device.

1.9Newsletters

Choose Newsletters in the footer or in your account to see the outlet's newsletters for each edition. Select the ones you want and enter your e-mail address. You will receive a message asking you to confirm: your subscription starts only when you select the link in it. Every newsletter has an unsubscribe link at the bottom and a link to manage all your newsletters.

1.10Notifications

On the website and in the apps, the outlet can send you notifications about breaking news and topics you follow. Your browser or phone asks you once whether to allow them. Choose Notifications to pick which editions and topics you want to hear about, or to turn them off.

1.11Supporting the outlet and memberships

Select Support to give once or to become a member. Members may get benefits such as stories without limits, no advertising, or newsletters only for members, depending on the outlet.

  • Prices are shown in your currency where the outlet offers it.
  • You can pay by card or through local services such as telebirr, Chapa, ArifPay, PayPal or a bank transfer, depending on what the outlet accepts. Payments go directly to the outlet; Sham MediaOS does not keep your card details.
  • Your membership, receipts and the date of the next payment are in Account → Membership, where you can also cancel.

If you see a limit on stories

Some outlets let you read a number of stories each month for free, and then ask you to sign in or become a member. Stories shared with you by a member, breaking news and public-interest stories may stay free; this depends on the outlet's choices.

1.12Comments

Where comments are open, you can join the discussion under a story after signing in. Comments follow the outlet's community rules. Depending on the outlet, a comment may appear at once or after a moderator has approved it. Use Report on a comment that breaks the rules; moderators review every report.

1.13Sending a tip, contributing and answering callouts

  • Tips let you send information to the newsroom. Use the secure form on the Tips page; add documents or photos if you have them. The page explains how the outlet protects sources. If you need to stay anonymous, do not use your work device or network.
  • Callouts are requests from journalists for experiences, photos or documents about a subject. Answer through the form on the callout page; the form tells you how your answer will be used and asks for your consent.
  • Contribute and Write for us explain how to offer stories, photos or video to the outlet and how contributors are paid, where the outlet has a contributor programme.

1.14Corrections, complaints and transparency

The outlet publishes its Corrections in one place, and every corrected story carries a note. If you think something is wrong or unfair, use Complaints to tell the outlet; you will get a reference and an answer. Report content is for content that is illegal or harmful. The Transparency page explains who owns and funds the outlet, and the AI policy explains if and how the outlet uses artificial intelligence.

1.15Slow connections, low data and offline reading

  • The light edition (/en/lite, /am/lite and so on) shows the latest stories as plain, very small pages that load on 2G connections and use little data.
  • After your first visit, the site keeps recent pages on your device so that you can read them offline. A note at the top tells you when you are reading a saved copy.
  • Where the outlet offers SMS or USSD, you can get headlines on a basic phone by sending a keyword to the outlet's number or by dialling its short code. The outlet publishes the number and keyword on its contacts page.
  • If the outlet's usual address cannot be reached in your country, it may publish alternative addresses and messaging channels on social media and in its newsletters. Its resilience pages work even when the main site is blocked.

1.16The apps

The outlet's own apps for Android, iPhone and iPad, and Windows are in Google Play, the App Store and the Microsoft Store under the outlet's name. The apps offer the same editions as the website, with notifications, saved stories, offline reading, audio and video.

To start using the app

  1. Install the app from your phone's store. Search for the outlet's name.
  2. Open it and choose your edition.
  3. Allow notifications if you want breaking news alerts.
  4. Sign in with the same e-mail address or phone number as on the website to see your saved stories, follows and membership.

1.17Privacy

The outlet decides what it collects and publishes a privacy policy. Sham MediaOS is built to collect little: the website does not use advertising trackers from other companies unless the outlet switches them on, and analytics are measured without following you across other sites. You can:

  • choose what you agree to the first time you sign in, and change it under Account → Privacy;
  • download a copy of your data;
  • delete your account;
  • unsubscribe from any newsletter with one click.

1.18Accessibility

The website and apps are designed for everyone. They work with screen readers, keyboard navigation and larger text; images carry descriptions; videos can carry captions; and colours meet contrast guidelines. If something is hard to use, tell the outlet through its contacts page.

1.19Questions readers often ask

I did not receive the sign-in e-mail. Check the spam folder and that the address is spelled correctly. Wait a minute and ask for a new message; the old link stops working when you ask for a new one.

The site shows the wrong language. Choose your language from the language menu. Your choice is remembered on this device.

My payment went through but I am not a member. Some payment methods confirm a few minutes later. If your membership does not appear within an hour, contact the outlet with your receipt; it can see the payment and activate your membership.

How do I stop notifications? In the website, choose Notifications and turn them off, or block notifications for the site in your browser settings. In the app, use your phone's settings.

How do I cancel my membership? Go to Account → Membership → Cancel. You keep your benefits until the end of the period you paid for.

Can I read without an account? Yes. You need an account only to save, follow, comment and manage newsletters and memberships.
