1.3People and roles

Roles

A role is a set of permissions. Sham MediaOS comes with ready-made roles in four groups. Role catalogue lists all of them with their permissions. Examples:

Group Roles
Executive Executive, Executive Administrator, Director, Editor-in-Chief
Editorial Managing Editor, Senior Editor, Section Editor, Desk Editor, Bureau Chief, Copy Editor, Reporter, Correspondent, Investigative Reporter, Photographer, Videographer, Producer, Fact Checker, Researcher, Translator, Legal Counsel, Standards Editor, Community Moderator, Newsletter Editor, Social Media Editor, Elections & Data Editor, Executive Producer, Broadcast Producer, Radio Producer, Presenter
Technical System Administrator, Security Administrator, DevOps, Developer, Support, Studio Operator, Data Protection Officer
Commercial Advertising Manager, Sales Manager, Partnership Manager, Reader Revenue Manager

The first administrator created by the installer is an Executive Administrator, which holds every permission.

Roles marked (MFA) in the role list require two-factor authentication. Examples are Editor-in-Chief, Managing Editor, System Administrator, Security Administrator, DevOps, Investigative Reporter, Legal Counsel and Reader Revenue Manager. A person with such a role must enrol at the next sign-in.

Scopes

You grant a role with a scope. The scope limits where the role applies:

Scope The role applies to
global Everything
language One language edition
desk One desk
bureau One bureau
section One section
country One country

For example, a Translator with the scope "language: Amharic" can translate only into the Amharic edition.

To add a staff account

  1. Open People & roles.

  2. Under Add a staff account, enter the Full name, Work email, a Temporary password (12 or more characters, mixed) and the Interface language.

  3. Select Create account.

  4. Send the temporary password to the person through a separate channel, for example by phone.

  5. Grant at least one role (below). A person with no roles cannot do anything yet.

To grant a role

  1. Open People & roles and open the person.

  2. Select + Grant a role.

  3. Choose the role. Roles you cannot grant are shown but disabled. You can only grant roles whose permissions you hold yourself.

  4. Choose the Scope and, if needed, which edition, desk, bureau, section or country.

  5. To give temporary access, set Expires.

  6. Select Grant.

Your licence limits the number of staff seats. The limit is checked when you grant a role. See Licence.

To remove access

Action Effect
Revoke (next to a role) Takes one role away
Suspend Blocks sign-in for now. Reactivate undoes it.
Offboard... For a person who leaves. Choose what happens to the account (Disable or Suspend) and Hand open work to a colleague.

In People & roles you can filter by status (Active, Suspended, Disabled) and by role. Labels such as No MFA and Locked out show accounts that need attention.

To change workflow rules per desk

  1. Open Workflow policies.

  2. Choose the desk.

  3. You can switch off the optional routes from review to fact check or to translation, or make comments or two-factor authentication required for more steps.
  4. Save.

The publication, correction and recovery steps always stay. Every change is recorded in the audit log.

1.4Editions and languages

Editions

An edition is one language version of your outlet, with its own address, front page, sections and stories. Your licence limits how many editions and languages you can use.

Open Administration → Editions to see every edition. For each edition you can set:

Field Meaning
Code (BCP-47) The language code, for example am or ar
English name, Native name How the edition is named
Script, Direction The writing system and left-to-right or right-to-left
Search stemming How search treats word forms in this language
Menu order The position in the edition switcher
Reader launch state Live, Preview or Hidden (below)
Launch state What readers see
Live Listed in the edition switcher, sitemaps and the apps; indexed by search engines
Preview Reachable by its address with a "Preview edition, coming soon" notice; not listed and not indexed
Hidden Readers get "not found". The newsroom keeps working on it.

The default edition is always live. Taking an edition offline hides it from readers; its content and history are kept.

Several brands in one installation (Enterprise)

On the Enterprise and Global editions, an edition can be its own publication. In Edition structure (Identity), give it its own publication name and logo, and if you like its own colour, description and share image. Its website then shows that name, logo and colour, and its e-mails to readers and staff carry its name and colour. Each brand lives at its edition's address (for example /so); a separate domain per brand is not supported yet.

On other editions the colour, description and share image can still be set, but a separate name or logo cannot.

To add an edition

  1. Open Administration → Editions.

  2. Under Add an edition, fill in the code, names, script and direction.

  3. Save. New editions start offline.

  4. Translate the interface strings (below) and publish a first set of stories.

  5. Set Reader launch state to Preview to test, then to Live.

Each edition's own site name, logo and contact details are set in Edition structure (Identity) in the Editions & publishing group.

Interface languages

The interface is the text around the journalism: menus, buttons, notices and e-mails. Sham MediaOS ships with 33 interface languages.

Status Languages
Shipped and in use English, Amharic, Afaan Oromoo, Somali, Tigrinya, Arabic, Kiswahili, French, Spanish, Turkish, Chinese
Machine drafts awaiting native review (22) German, Dutch, Portuguese, Italian, Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Greek, Hindi, Bengali, Urdu, Persian, Hebrew, Thai, Japanese, Korean, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Hausa, Yoruba, Zulu, Kinyarwanda

The 22 machine drafts were made with AI and checked for completeness, placeholders and plural forms. They ship as prepared, inactive editions. Before you launch one of them, a native-speaker editor should review it.

To review an interface language

  1. Open Interface translations (in the Editions & publishing group). You can also reach it from the link on Administration → Editions.

  2. Choose the language. The page shows how many strings are translated.

  3. Search and correct the strings. Missing strings fall back to English.

  4. Ask the translators to read the notes in docs/i18n/MACHINE-DRAFT-REVIEW.md. They list choices worth checking for each language, for example the form of address and SMS keywords.

The coverage percentage counts translated strings only. It does not mean the edition is editorially ready.

What read-only means

  • Staff can sign in and read everything, but cannot save, publish or change anything.
  • Readers are never affected. The website, apps and feeds keep running.
  • Administration → Licence always stays open, so you can always install a new key.

Check-ins

The installation checks in with Sham's licence server once a day. A licence works on one installation only. The licence server binds it to the first installation that checks in with it.

A check-in sends only: the licence ID, the installation's random ID, the host names it answers on, the version, and counts (staff, editions, languages, studios). No content, reader data or personal data leaves your server.

If your server was offline, select Check in now on the Licence page once the connection is back. Sites that can never reach the internet can ask Sham for an offline licence, which needs no check-ins.

Limits

  • Staff seats are checked when you grant a role.
  • Studios are checked when you create a studio.

  • Editions and languages in use are shown beside the limits of your edition.

  • Desks outside your edition (for example podcasts or the AI desk on Starter) show "not in your edition" with a link to this page. Readers are never affected.

If you need more seats, studios, editions or languages, upgrade at store.sham.et and install the new key.

To get help with the licence

Quote the Installation ID and Licence ID from This installation when you write to Sham at contact@sham.et. The Licence page also links to the store and to Sham's sales address.

1.9Backups

Sham MediaOS makes encrypted backups by itself:

When (UTC) What
Every day, 01:30 The database
Every Sunday, 02:00 Database, media and the settings file
Every Sunday, 05:30 An automatic restore test into a scratch database

The Docker installation also has a second, independent backup of the database and the media files every night, with an optional off-site copy. See the Installation Guide see Installation and Operations Manual.

Keep the backup password safe. Backups are encrypted with BACKUP_ARCHIVE_PASSWORD. Without it they cannot be restored. Keep it in your password manager, with two people able to reach it.

The Backup Center

Open Administration → Backup Center. It shows the schedule, the backups, their checks, and warnings such as "Latest backup is old" or "Only one destination".

Permission Allows
backup.view See the backups and history
backup.manage Back up now and Verify an archive
backup.restore Request, approve, reject or cancel restores

To make a backup now

  1. Open Backup Center.

  2. Under Back up now, choose Database only (fast) or Full: database + media + configuration.

  3. Select Start backup.

The backup runs in the background within a minute. Only one backup of each kind runs at a time.

Verify opens a copy of an archive, decrypts it and checks the database dump. Nothing live is touched.

To restore (four-eyes)

A restore always needs two administrators.

  1. The first administrator opens Request a restore, chooses the archive and What to restore, writes the Reason (recorded in the audit log) and types RESTORE to confirm. Then selects Request restore.

  2. A different administrator approves it. Nobody can approve their own request.

  3. What happens next depends on the scope:

Scope What happens
media Missing media files are copied back. Existing files are never overwritten. Runs by itself.
config The old settings file is placed beside the live one for comparison. Only the names of changed settings are shown.
database Replaces the live database. Runs only from the server (advanced, below).
full Database, then media and configuration. Runs only from the server.

To run an approved database restore (advanced)

  1. On the server, change to infra/docker.

  2. Run docker compose exec api php artisan jmn:backup:restore <restore-id>. The ID is shown in the Backup Center.

  3. Type the ID again when asked.

The command checks the approval, verifies the archive, takes a safety backup of the current database, puts the site into maintenance mode, restores, runs the database updates, and brings the site back. If anything fails, the safety backup is the way back. Afterwards, run docker compose exec api php artisan jmn:audit:verify.

1.10Health, operations and the go-live checklist

System health

System health shows every check: database, Redis, queues, scheduler, disk, mail, backups, the digital newspaper fonts and more.

  • OK: the part works.
  • Degraded: an optional part is missing or not set up, for example an optional service.
  • Critical or failed: fix it now. For example, the scheduler has not run for more than 5 minutes.

If a queue stops working, System health shows "queue stalled" and the Command centre shows an alarm.

Operations Center

Operations Center shows pending and failed jobs per queue, media that is stuck, and the state of webhook, social and newsletter delivery. People with ops.manage (System Administrator, DevOps) can run safe recovery actions with a session confirmed with two-factor authentication:

Action Effect
Retry failed jobs The jobs run again
Discard failed job records Deletes the records only
Release stale locks Stuck deliveries become due again. Social posts stuck while sending are marked failed for a person to check; they are never re-sent by themselves.
Retry webhooks Failed webhook deliveries run again
Rebuild caches The website refreshes every edition. Nothing is deleted.
Regenerate media Missing image sizes are made again
Retry stuck media Stuck images are processed again

None of these actions edits, publishes or deletes journalism.

1.11Security

Two-factor authentication

  • Roles marked (MFA) must use an authenticator app. People enrol at the first sign-in.
  • Every person gets 10 recovery codes at enrolment.
  • Sensitive actions (breaking alerts, backups, restores, keys, refunds, and others) need a session confirmed with the authenticator code.
  • If a person loses their phone and their recovery codes, an administrator can reset two-step sign-in for them.

Your outlet can also switch on passkeys. Staff sign-in with Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra ID or a SAML 2.0 identity provider (single sign-on) is part of the Enterprise and Global editions; on other editions the buttons do not appear.

Single sign-on

Single sign-on replaces only the password. Lockout, the authenticator code for (MFA) roles, sessions and the audit log work exactly as with a password. Only existing, active staff accounts can sign in: single sign-on never creates an account. The first time a person signs in, the account is linked to that identity; afterwards only that identity opens the account, even if someone else is given the same e-mail address. To accept only your own addresses, set JMN_STAFF_SSO_DOMAINS (for example example.com,example.org); this applies to every provider.

Google Workspace and Microsoft Entra ID (OpenID Connect) are set with JMN_STAFF_SSO_GOOGLE_* and JMN_STAFF_SSO_MICROSOFT_* in the server's .env file (each is explained in .env.example).

To set up SAML 2.0 (Okta, ADFS, Keycloak, Entra ID SAML and others) (advanced)

  1. Give your identity provider (IdP) this installation's metadata: https://<your API address>/api/v1/auth/saml/metadata. If the IdP cannot read a URL, enter by hand: - Entity ID / Audience: the same metadata address (or the value you set in JMN_STAFF_SSO_SAML_SP_ENTITY_ID); - ACS (single sign-on URL, Reply URL): https://<your API address>/api/v1/auth/saml/acs, binding HTTP-POST; - Name ID: persistent, or the e-mail address. Transient name IDs are refused, because the account is linked to the name ID; - an attribute with the person's e-mail address. Its name is email unless you set JMN_STAFF_SSO_SAML_EMAIL_ATTRIBUTE (ADFS and Entra ID often use http://schemas.xmlsoap.org/ws/2005/05/identity/claims/emailaddress); - sign the assertion or the response (or both) with RSA-SHA256; SHA-1 is refused; - do not encrypt the assertion. Encrypted assertions are not supported; staff then see a message asking you to turn encryption off.

  2. From the IdP, copy its entity ID (issuer), its single sign-on URL (HTTP-Redirect) and its signing certificate.

  3. Put them in the server's .env: JMN_STAFF_SSO_SAML_IDP_ENTITY_ID, JMN_STAFF_SSO_SAML_IDP_SSO_URL (https) and JMN_STAFF_SSO_SAML_IDP_CERT (the PEM certificate; write line breaks as \n, or give only the base64 part). Instead of the certificate text you can give a file with JMN_STAFF_SSO_SAML_IDP_CERT_FILE. During a certificate change, put both certificates in the file; either is accepted.

  4. Restart the API. Sign in with SAML appears on the newsroom sign-in page.

Sign-in always starts from the newsroom sign-in page; starting it from the IdP's portal (IdP-initiated) is refused. Only the configured certificate is trusted, whatever the response carries. Failed attempts are recorded as security events (sso_failed, with the reason). If you move to another IdP, the existing SAML links no longer match. Staff then sign in with their password until the old links are removed on the server: docker compose exec api php artisan tinker --execute="DB::table('staff_sso_identities')->where('provider', 'saml')->delete();".

Sign-in protection

Five wrong passwords lock out that address for that account for 15 minutes. The account itself stays usable from a clean address. When an account collects 20 failures in 15 minutes, a high-severity security alert opens and the account owner gets an e-mail.

Audit log

Audit log records who did what and when: sign-ins, workflow moves, breaking alerts, role changes, key changes, backups, restores and more. Entries cannot be changed or deleted. Each entry is chained to the one before it, so a change to the history can be detected. The Overview page shows the state of the audit chain.

To check the whole chain on the server (advanced): docker compose exec api php artisan jmn:audit:verify.

To export the audit log for an auditor (Enterprise)

  1. Sign in with your authenticator code (the export needs a verified session).

  2. Open Administration → Audit log and open Export for auditors.

  3. Choose From, To and the format: CSV for spreadsheets or JSON Lines for tools.

  4. Select Download.

A file holds at most 50,000 entries; for a longer period, export it in parts. Every entry carries its own hash and the hash before it. The file is signed by your installation, and the signature is part of the file name (audit-20261101-20261130.sig-….csv), so keep the name as it is. To prove later that a file was not changed, copy it to the server and run docker compose exec api php artisan jmn:audit:verify-export <file>. Each export is itself recorded in the audit log.

If you suspect a break-in (advanced)

  1. Sign out every session: docker compose exec api php artisan jmn:security:revoke-all.

  2. Change the server secrets. See "Key rotation" in the Installation Guide see Installation and Operations Manual.

  3. Ask staff to reset their passwords.

  4. Run jmn:audit:verify and review the security events.

Routines and security checklists

What to check every day, week, month and year, and how to keep the system secure.

2.1  Every day (5 minutes)2.2  Every week (30 minutes)2.3  Every month (2 hours)2.4  Every year2.5  Security hardening checklist2.6  When someone leaves2.7  When you suspect a break-in2.8  Data protection requests

A well-run installation needs a few minutes of attention every day and a short session every week, month and year. This chapter lists what to check, where, and what to do when something is wrong.

2.1Every day (5 minutes)

  • Open Administration → System health. Every line should be green. Amber means degraded but working; red needs action today.
  • Open Operations Center. The queues should be close to empty; failed jobs should be zero or explained.
  • Check that last night's backup finished and was copied off-site (Backup Center).
  • Glance at the licence state in the health page: Active, with a recent check-in.
  • Read any e-mail the system sent to administrators overnight (failed backups, webhook endpoints switched off, certificates expiring).

2.2Every week (30 minutes)

  • People: review accounts created or changed this week. Remove access for anyone who left (use Offboard, not just suspend).
  • Audit log: filter on sensitive actions (role grants, key changes, restores, unpublish, exports) and make sure each one is expected.
  • Newsletters → Deliverability: bounce and complaint rates within limits.
  • Webhooks: no endpoint switched off for failures.
  • Storage: free disk space above 25 %; media storage growth as expected.
  • Updates: Administration shows whether a signed update is available. Plan updates for a quiet time.

2.3Every month (2 hours)

  • Test restore: restore last night's backup to a test server, or use the automatic restore test, and record the result. A backup that has never been restored is not a backup.
  • Roles review: list everyone with administration, publishing to all editions, or breaking-news permissions. Confirm each still needs it.
  • Two-step sign-in: confirm everyone with a sensitive role has it set up (the People page shows who does not).
  • Keys and connections: check which keys expire soon (payment providers, store accounts, certificates).
  • Interface translations: review new or changed strings in each language.
  • Capacity: compare peak load and queue times with last month (Operations Center).

2.4Every year

  • Renew the licence before its expiry date (the licence page and e-mails warn 30 days ahead).
  • Rotate secrets that your policy says to rotate: application key procedure, database password, API keys of outside services (see Installation and Operations Manual, key rotation).
  • Run a disaster recovery exercise: rebuild the system on a new server from backups and time it.
  • Review the privacy policy, terms and AI policy pages with your lawyer.
  • Re-assess resilience: are the mirrors and alternative channels still working?
  • Commission or repeat a penetration test if your risk requires it.

2.6When someone leaves

Offboarding

  1. Open People, find the person and select Offboard.
  2. Choose who takes over their open stories, assignments and tasks.
  3. Confirm with your two-step code. The person is signed out on every device, their roles are removed and their tokens revoked. Their published bylines stay.
  4. If they had access to outside services (social accounts, store accounts, payment dashboards), change those passwords too; Sham MediaOS cannot do that for you.

2.7When you suspect a break-in

Act quickly, keep evidence

  1. Do not delete anything. Logs and the audit chain are evidence.
  2. Sign out all sessions of the affected accounts (People → the person → Sign out everywhere) and reset their sign-in.
  3. If an administrator account is affected, rotate the keys in Keys and connections and at each provider.
  4. Export the audit log for the period.
  5. Check the audit log for role grants, key changes, exports and unpublished or changed stories.
  6. Contact your hosting provider and, if personal data may have been exposed, your data protection officer: the law may require you to notify the authority and the people affected within a set time.
  7. Contact Sham support with the time window and what you saw.

2.8Data protection requests

Readers can download and delete their own data from their account. Requests that come by letter or e-mail are handled under Administration → Compliance: record the request, verify the person's identity, act within the legal deadline, and record what you did.
